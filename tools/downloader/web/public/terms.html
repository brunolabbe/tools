Terms, and what this service records

Draft. The operator has not yet approved this text. Delete this paragraph when they do.

What it is for

You give this service the address of a web page. It looks for the video the page plays and, if you ask, streams that video to you once, as a file, through a link that works one time and expires fifteen minutes after it is made.

Use it for video you have the right to save: your own, video its owner offers for saving, or video the law where you live lets you keep. What you ask it to fetch is your responsibility. Do not use it to put load on someone else's site, and do not use it to reach anything that is not public.

The one thing it will not do

It does not get around copy protection. When a video is protected by DRM (Widevine, PlayReady or FairPlay) it says so and stops. It never tries to obtain a key or a licence.

No video is stored

Each file is passed to the person who opened its link while it is being prepared, and no copy is written on the server. A preview image, when there is one, is held in the server's memory for ten minutes and then dropped.

What it records, and for how long

There are no accounts. Three things are written down on the server, and they are kept for different lengths of time.

  • The server's log, about 14 days. Every request writes one line: the time, what was asked of this service, how it ended, how long it took, and the network address it came from. A line about a failed request, or about a page that was analysed, can also hold that page's domain and path, never anything after a ?. For a failed request that line shares a request number with the line holding the address. The log is kept in files of a fixed size, and the oldest file is discarded when they are full, so "about 14 days" is what ordinary use fills; a quiet service keeps its lines longer.
  • A record for each download you start, 14 days. It holds the full address of the page you gave, including anything after a ?, the options you chose, and how the download ended. It is deleted 14 days after it was made.
  • A record of how each analysis ended, 90 days. It holds the page's domain only, never its path or anything after a ?, and never your network address. It also holds which methods were tried and how long each took. It is kept for 90 days, a length the operator can change.

Put together, the log and the download record let the operator work out which address asked for which page while both still exist. That is what answers a report of abuse, and the logs are read for that, and to run the service and set its limits.

Your browser keeps your list of downloads and your light or dark choice in its own storage on your device. The service does not read them. The service is reached through Cloudflare, which handles the connection and keeps its own records under its own terms; when the check that you are a person is switched on, Cloudflare runs it in your browser.

If something should be stopped

If you hold the rights to a video, or run a site that is being fetched through this service, write to abuse@oludoi.com. Say which page or site, and what you want stopped. A person reads it.
