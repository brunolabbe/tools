Terms, and what this service records

What it is for

You give this service the address of a web page. It looks for the video the page plays and, if you ask, streams that video to you once, as a file, through a link that works one time and expires fifteen minutes after it is made.

Use it for video you have the right to save: your own, video its owner offers for saving, or video the law where you live lets you keep. What you ask it to fetch is your responsibility. Do not use it to put load on someone else's site, and do not use it to reach anything that is not public.

The one thing it will not do

It does not get around copy protection. When a video is protected by DRM (Widevine, PlayReady or FairPlay) it says so and stops. The service's own code never asks for a key or a licence.

No video is stored

Each file is passed to the person who opened its link while it is being prepared, and no copy is written on the server. A preview image, when there is one, is held in the server's memory for ten minutes and then dropped.

What it records, and for how long

There are no accounts. Three things are written down on the server, and they are kept for different lengths of time.

  • The server's log, about 7 days. Each request, apart from the host's routine health check, writes one line when it finishes: the time, what was asked of this service, how it ended, how long it took, and the network it came from. That is a range, never your whole address: the first three parts of an IPv4 address, or the first three groups of an IPv6 one. A line about a failed request, or about a page that was analysed, can also hold that page's domain, never its path or anything after it. For a failed request that line shares a request number with the line holding the network. The log is kept in files of a fixed size, and the oldest file is discarded when they are full, so "about 7 days" is what ordinary use fills; a quiet service keeps its lines longer. It is read to run the service, to set its limits and to follow up a report of abuse.
  • A record for each download you start, 14 days. While the download runs, and for 15 minutes after it ends, it holds the full address of the page you gave, including anything after a ?, the full address of the video file that was chosen, which can also carry a ? part, and the video's title as the file name. After those 15 minutes they are erased and the record keeps only the page's domain, the options you chose, how the download ended and when. It is deleted 14 days after it was made. It exists so the download can run, and so the operator can tell how often downloads fail and on which sites.
  • A record of how each analysis ended, 90 days. It holds the page's domain only, never its path or anything after a ?, and never your network address. It also holds which methods were tried and how long each took. It is kept for 90 days, a length the operator can change, and it exists to show which sites the service handles well.

The limits that slow one visitor down keep your address in the server's memory (for IPv6, its first 64 bits), for as long as it takes to count, and write it nowhere. Put together, then, the log can show that a burst of requests came from one network, and the download record can show which site was fetched and when. While a download runs, and for 15 minutes after, the request number in the log can tie a network to the page of that download; after that, neither says which page, and neither says which person.

Your browser keeps your list of downloads and your light or dark choice in its own storage on your device. The service does not read them. The service is reached through Cloudflare, a company based in the United States, which handles the connection and keeps its own records under its own terms; when the check that you are a person is switched on, Cloudflare runs it in your browser.

Who is responsible

The person in charge of the personal information described on this page is the operator of this service, one person, reached at abuse@oludoi.com, which a person reads. A question about what is recorded goes there too.

If something should be stopped

If you hold the rights to a video, or run a site that is being fetched through this service, write to abuse@oludoi.com. Say which page or site, and what you want stopped. A person reads it.

A legal notice, or an order to keep records, is answered with what exists on the day it arrives: the operator copies the log and the download records then. Nothing is kept ahead of time in case one comes.
