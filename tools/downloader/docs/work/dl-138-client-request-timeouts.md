# dl-138 — A probe or job request that never settles holds its slot

## Why

dl-133 (PR #781, branch `downloader/dl-133-one-box-per-analysis`, `ready` on main and not merged) gives each analysis its own box. The page counts its probes against the server's per-client probe cap (2 at once, `maxProbesPerClient`, `api/src/config.ts`), and its jobs against the job cap (2 in flight, `maxJobsPerClient`). For a closed analysis, the owner chose to count its probe POST until the POST settles, because the server holds its probe slot until its handler ends: `clientReleaseProbe` is released in the handler's outermost `finally` (`api/src/routes/probe.ts`). The job count is the page's own. The server takes no job slot at `POST /api/jobs`, which writes a `queued` row and hands back a link; the slot is taken when the link is opened (`api/src/routes/jobs.ts`, `routes/files.ts`, `jobs/links.ts`). Both counts assume that a request settles. On main, `request()` in `tools/downloader/web/src/api/http.ts` passes `fetch` no timeout and no abort signal, and `ApiClient.probe` and `ApiClient.createJob` both go through it, so a request that stalls keeps its count for the life of the page.

Gate 2 of dl-133 found two cases, both `low`, and neither has a live call site:

- **N2, probe.** Two running analyses were closed while their probe POSTs were out, and those POSTs never settle. The count stays at 2 for the life of the page, and every later analysis waits. `ApiClient.probe` also spans the human-check wait before the POST (`withHumanCheck`, awaited before `request()` is called), which holds no server slot, so the count over-holds there. That is the safe direction.
- **N3, createJob.** A `createJob` that never settles leaves its box `starting`. Download, Analyse again and Dismiss are all disabled, and the box keeps its job count (`jobsInFlight`, `web/src/lib/boxes.ts`, which counts a `probed` box whose download is `starting`). dl-133's gate 1 F2 fix disabled Analyse again while the job is being created, which removed the box's only way out.

Both have one cause: no client-side request timeout. Their remedies differed, and the owner chose them in the Decision below.

**Reproduction.** Gate 2's probe (`gate-dl133-g2-probe.test.tsx`, cases `(o2)` and `(n3)`) is in a session scratch directory and is not committed. It is reference only, and no `Done when` line depends on it. Each case drives the real `App` with a mocked `ApiClient` under fake timers, advances 30 minutes, and reads the page. The filer re-ran it at dl-133's head `1042c0c6`, which is not the `4c621dc8` that gate 2 read: the branch has moved on since. Both shas are named as the readings' provenance only; a squash merge of PR #781 strands them, and no line below pins either. Its lines, which gate 1 re-ran and got again:

- `(o2)` 30 min with two orphans out and two boxes waiting: `probe calls 2; headings Waiting | Waiting`
- `(o2)` three new analyses on a settled page: `started 2 of 3`, once the orphans have settled
- `(n3)` 30 min after Download with createJob unsettled, buttons in the box: `Starting…:true | Analyse again:true | Dismiss:true; createJob calls 1`

The probe replaces `ApiClient` with a fake, so it never reaches `request()` and cannot show a timeout. Its `(o2)` case also asserts the wedge (`expect(calls(fake)).toBe(2)`) before its later steps, so it cannot read a fix at all. A fix is graded by the committed App-level cases and `ApiClient`-level tests named in Done when.

## Decision — answered 2026-11-10 by the owner

Each question was put as options, the recommended one first. The recommendations were the filer's and the orchestrator's; the choices are the owner's.

**Question 1 — answered 2026-11-10 by the owner.** Record N2 and N3 only, or file a ticket. Options: record only (recommended by the orchestrator); file a ticket. Chosen: file a ticket. This ticket is that filing.

**Question 2 — answered 2026-11-10 by the owner.** The remedy for N2, `ApiClient.probe`, a probe POST that never settles. Options:

- timeout at 90 s (recommended);
- timeout at 65 s;
- abort on close, and start a waiting box at once;
- abort on close, and hold a waiting box for a fixed delay.

Chosen: **a client timeout on the probe POST, timed from the fetch, at 90 s.** The timer starts at the fetch, after `withHumanCheck`, so the human-check wait on the page is not counted. A settled POST is what dl-133's count rule expects, so the rule does not change.

Why 90 s, from the floor. The bound must clear the longest the server can take to release its probe slot, measured from that same moment, or the count gives the slot back while the server still holds it: F1's mismatch again. **The floor is measured from the fetch and includes the waits before the slot is taken**, because the slot is not taken at the fetch. In `routes/probe.ts` the handler first awaits `humanCheck.require` (the siteverify, bounded at `SITEVERIFY_TIMEOUT_MS`, 5 s, `api/src/human-check.ts`, when the check is on; whether the live deployment has it on was not checked), then `guard.assertAllowed` (one `dns.lookup`, `ssrf.ts`, with no bound in the code read), then reads the cache, and only then calls `probeClientGate.tryAcquire`. The slot is then held through `registry.resolve` (`probeTimeoutMs`, 45 s by default, `PROBE_TIMEOUT_CEILING_MS` 50 s, `config.ts`), then `guard.assertAllAllowed` (the vet of the resolver's output: it runs before the frame grab, and it runs one lookup per distinct URL concurrently, none bounded in the code read), then `captureThumbnail`, whose frame grab is bounded at `FRAME_GRAB_TIMEOUT_MS` (6 s, `thumbnails.ts`). It is released in the handler's outermost `finally`. By arithmetic from the code, not measured: the slot is held about 51 s at the default and about 56 s at the ceiling, plus the vet's lookups; from the fetch, add the siteverify's 5 s and `assertAllowed`'s lookup, which makes the release as late as about 56 s at the default and about 61 s at the ceiling on a deployment with the check on. Those figures are the bounded terms only, and the unbounded waits on the usual path are three: `assertAllowed`, the vet's `assertAllAllowed`, and, where the source names an image, a third `guard.assertAllowed` that `captureThumbnail` awaits before its 4 s fetch (the 6 s frame grab runs only when no image is named, and the two never stack, so 56 s and 61 s are the larger path). A bound of 60 s would therefore have cleared the default by 4 s over the bounded terms only, and not the ceiling. The chosen 90 s clears the ceiling's 61 s by 29 s over the bounded terms; it does not bound the unbounded lookups. Not counted and not measured: the request's transit and upload before the handler, `return await reply.send` until the response is written, and the resolvers' unwind after the chain's deadline signal fires. `probeTimeoutMs` is the resolution chain's budget, not the whole hold, and the page cannot read the deployment's value: `clientConfigResponseSchema` carries only `humanCheck` (`contract/src/api.ts`).

Costs the owner accepted: after a closed analysis, a count can be held up to 90 s; and the floor cannot be checked from the page, only by judgement. **Unmeasured (gate 2's direction, the server not run):** if the timeout aborts the fetch, the socket closes and the server's slot frees only when its handler unwinds, so a bound below the floor would leave a window in which a third analysis meets `RATE_LIMITED`, and the floor is not a hard limit; the handler registers its `close` listener after the siteverify, the lookup and the cache read, so an abort in that first phase may not shorten the hold. At 90 s the bound is above the floor, so the question matters less, and it was not put to the owner: whether the timeout aborts the fetch or only stops waiting on it is left to the builder, who names the choice in the Log.

Not chosen, kept as the options the owner chose from:

- **Timeout at 65 s.** The same shape with a margin of 4 s over the 61 s figure.
- **Abort on close, start a waiting box at once.** The option dl-133's F1 passed over. It needs no bound to name, and it frees the client's count at once, but the server's slot frees only when its handler unwinds after the socket closes, so a window remains in which a third analysis could be refused `RATE_LIMITED`, the F1 symptom. It touches `api/types.ts`, `web/src/api/http.ts`, `mock.ts` and `useBoxes`.
- **Abort on close, hold a waiting box for a fixed delay.** The same abort, and a delay constant the owner would name.

**Question 3 — answered 2026-11-10 by the owner.** The remedy for N3, `createJob`, a request that never answers. Options:

- timeout at 30 s (recommended);
- Dismiss enabled while the job is being created, with a guard on a late answer;
- both.

Chosen: **a client timeout on `createJob`, at 30 s.** A timed-out `createJob` leaves one `queued` row that nothing opens. The page's `useJobs.start` throws before `attach`, so `openLink` is never called and no box is re-created. The row takes no job slot, because the slot is taken at the link's `GET` (`routes/files.ts`). The sweep cancels an unopened row as `link-expired` after the 15-minute link TTL (`LINK_TTL_MS`, `jobs/links.ts`; `runSweep` in `server.ts` runs every `gcIntervalMs`, 60 s by default). The row spends one token of the per-minute `POST /api/jobs` limiter. The box shows the request's error (`request()` maps a failed fetch to `UNREACHABLE`), and its buttons are enabled again. The POST's own waits are the human-check siteverify, bounded at 5 s (`SITEVERIFY_TIMEOUT_MS`, `api/src/human-check.ts`), and the SSRF guard's `dns.lookup` (`api/src/ssrf.ts`), which has no bound in the code read; 30 s clears the first by 25 s and does not bound the second. Costs the owner accepted: a visitor who retries after the failure leaves a second queued row.

Not chosen, kept as the options the owner chose from:

- **Dismiss enabled while the job is being created, with a guard on a late answer.** It reverses the rule that dl-133 keeps, that Dismiss is disabled while a job is created (`ProbePanel.tsx`), and the committed test that asserts it, `web/test/probe-panel.test.tsx` › "an analysis is dismissed from its own panel, except while its job is being created (dl-133)". It needs a guard on the late answer, or a dismissed box is re-created and its download starts. A `createJob` which never answers still leaves its request pending.
- **Both.**

**Question 4 — answered 2026-11-10 by the owner.** Where the timeouts live. Options: on the two calls only; in `request()`, for every call. Chosen: **on the two calls only**, `ApiClient.probe` and `ApiClient.createJob`. `request()` is unchanged, so `GET /api/config`, `getJob` and `cancelJob` gain no bound.
