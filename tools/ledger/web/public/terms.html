Terms, and what this service keeps

What it is for

The ledger is a household's shared account: the bank statements its members paste in, the receipts they file, who put what into the mortgage and the buffer, and who owes whom. It is for the households whose addresses the operator has admitted, and for nobody else. Use it for your own household's money.

What it keeps

Everything is kept on one disk volume on the operator's server. There is no other copy unless the operator makes a backup. Three kinds of thing are on it.

  • The directory. Which households exist and what each is called, the email address of every member and the name the household shows for them, who added or removed each address and when, and the moment each address first signed in.
  • Each household's books, one file per household. The statement rows you paste (date, description, amount and balance), the rules and categories you set, the salaries and ratios, the periods and the lines in them, the receipts you file, and the household's word on its mortgage. Every record carries the person it was filed by.
  • Receipt photos, in a folder per household beside the books, each under a random name.

Your browser keeps one cookie of ours, webtools-theme, which says only whether you chose a light or a dark look. Cloudflare sets its own cookies when it signs you in.

Who can read a household's books

  • Only someone signed in. The ledger sits behind Cloudflare Access, and on top of that it checks the signed token Access attaches on every request to its data, and refuses a request without a valid one. Cloudflare, a company based in the United States, carries the connection and keeps its own records under its own terms.
  • Every member of the household, and no one from another. An address the directory does not hold is refused, with a page saying it belongs to no household. A member reads and writes the whole of their household's books: there are no private parts inside a household, and any member can add another address to it.
  • The admin sees the directory, not the books. The admin, who is named in the server's configuration, sees every household's name, its members, their email addresses and when each first signed in, and can add and rename households and members and remove an address. That does not open a household's books to them. They read those only as a member of that household.
  • Whoever runs the server can read the files. The books are ordinary database files, not encrypted, and the operator has the host they are on. The operator can also run the commands that import a spreadsheet into a household or change who belongs where.
  • A model, only when asked, and only if the operator set a key. When a member taps "Read it" on a receipt, that photo is sent to Anthropic, a company based in the United States, to be read. When a member asks for a spreadsheet to be described, a sample of its rows is sent: the first rows, a few from the middle and the last, with long text cut short. Without a key the ledger sends nothing outside the server and a receipt is typed in by hand. What Anthropic keeps is under its own terms.

How long it is kept, and how a household leaves

It is kept for as long as the volume is. There is no expiry, and nothing deletes it. The ledger never edits or deletes a stored record: a correction is a new record that supersedes the old one, and the old one stays. The directory is built the same way, and its database refuses an edit or a deletion.

There is no screen or command that deletes a household, its books or a receipt photo, and this page does not promise one. What an admin can do is remove an address from a household, which ends that address's access at once; the record that it was a member stays. A person can also be retired from the books at the next close, which stops counting them from then on and keeps what was filed under their name. If a household wants its data gone, ask the operator, who would have to do it by hand on the server, outside the ledger.

What the server's log holds

The log records the server starting and stopping, requests that were refused, and errors. A refused request writes the method, the address it asked for, and how it ended. A refused sign-in of an address that Access admitted but the directory does not hold writes that email address. A request refused for going too fast writes the household and the person's id. It holds no statement row, no amount, no receipt and no photo. The log is kept in a few files of fixed size, and the oldest is discarded when they fill, so this page promises no length of time.

Who is responsible

The person in charge of the information described here is the operator of this service. This page gives no address for them: ask whoever added your email to your household.
